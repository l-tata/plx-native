# v0.9.0 — Live TV from your own Tunarr server, and a way back to the show

This release adds Live TV: channels and a TV guide from a [Tunarr](https://github.com/chrisbenincasa/tunarr) server on your home network. It also adds a Go to Show button on episode pages, a clock, more patience with large files that stall, and Home rows that stay current.

## What's new

- **Live TV from Tunarr.** Settings → Live TV searches your home network for a Tunarr server, or takes its address if the search finds nothing. Once one is set up, a **Live TV** tab appears beside Search, with a guide of what is on now and for the next day. OK on a programme tunes its channel, and the guide opens on the channel you watched last. While a channel plays, CH▲ / CH▼ change channel, the number buttons go straight to a channel, Left returns to the channel you were on before, and a banner shows the channel, the programme, how far through it is and what is next. Up / Down open a channel list over the picture so you can see what is on elsewhere while your channel keeps playing; OK switches to the highlighted channel and BACK closes the list. A channel that drops is tuned again by itself, up to three times. This uses only Tunarr, the way Plex and Jellyfin use it: it needs no Plex Pass and nothing from your Plex server, and it is not Plex's own Live TV or its free channels.
- **Go to Show on an episode's page.** An episode's page now ends its button row with **Go to Show**, which opens the show on that episode's season. This is the way back to the show from an episode you opened from Continue Watching, where BACK takes you Home.
- **A clock.** The time shows at the top right of Home, your libraries, Search and Live TV, on your television's 12- or 24-hour setting.
- **Home stays current.** Home asks your server for its rows again once they are 15 minutes old, so Continue Watching and On Deck catch up with what was watched on another device, or while the television was asleep.

## Fixed

- **A large file playing directly could stop with an error when the connection stalled.** A video that streams straight from your server now reconnects at the same position and keeps trying for up to two minutes before it gives up.

## Compatibility

- **Plays video, verified by watching it — none for this version.** This version has not yet been run on a television. Live TV in particular has been tested only on a computer, against recorded Tunarr responses.
- **Starts, and nothing further is known — every other firmware from webOS 4.0 up is expected to start.** This binary's libraries and symbols are checked against the firmware images webosbrew has; the result for this build is in the [technical audit](https://github.com/l-tata/plx-native/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.9.0.md). That grades startup and says nothing about whether video plays.

## Known issues

- **A Tunarr channel whose frame rate changes partway through can stutter or stop.** The rate is read once when you tune. In Tunarr, turn on **Normalize frame rate** in the transcode settings so each channel keeps one rate.
- **The clock and the guide's times can be off by your time zone.** Some televisions do not tell apps their time zone. When the app sees UTC it asks the television's own clock service, and if that does not answer it uses the time zone your Tunarr server writes into its guide.
- **Home has no On Now row for Live TV yet.** The guide and the channel list are where live programmes show.

## Help test this release

**If you run Tunarr, try Live TV.** Nobody has watched it on a television yet. Tell us whether the search found your server, whether channels play, and whether the guide times are right. [Open an issue](https://github.com/l-tata/plx-native/issues). It working is as useful a report as it failing.

## Installing

Download **com.beb.plxnative_0.9.0_arm.ipk** and install it with the [Homebrew Channel](https://github.com/webosbrew/webos-homebrew-channel) or [dev-manager-desktop](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop). You do **not** need a rooted TV.

Nothing in this distribution chain is signed, so this sha256 is what tells you the file you have is the file published here:

```
__IPK_SHA256__  com.beb.plxnative_0.9.0_arm.ipk
```

[Installing and checking a download](https://github.com/l-tata/plx-native/blob/main/docs/install-and-verify.md) covers the other assets, how to check the hash on each platform, and the Developer Mode expiry that uninstalls your apps. This package bundles FFmpeg under LGPL-2.1-or-later and its complete corresponding source is attached to this release. Exactly what was built, verified and shipped is in the [technical audit for v0.9.0](https://github.com/l-tata/plx-native/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.9.0.md).
