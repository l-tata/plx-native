## Device test evidence

What was checked off the television: a stream made with FFmpeg 6.1 the way Tunarr makes one (libx264 with B-frames and access unit delimiters, AAC, MPEG-TS) was read by `plx_media::live::analyse` — H.264, AAC, 29.970 fps, both from its start and from a cut 3000 packets in — and all 180 of its video access units went through `ProgressiveVideo` unchanged, three of them keyframes. The packets FFmpeg's MPEG-TS demuxer hands the app were inspected with ffprobe: video begins `00 00 00 01 09` (Annex-B), audio `ff f1` (ADTS). Whether Starfish decodes that feed, and whether the rebased timestamps keep picture and sound in step, is unverified.

## Known issues at release, with provenance

- **The v0.9.0 Live TV stall, and why.** The progressive demuxer (`plx_media::ff::demux`) was written for MP4 and Matroska. MPEG-TS H.264/HEVC is Annex-B, and its AVCC rewrite read the first `00 00 00 01` as a NAL length of one, feeding the decoder one byte per frame; MPEG-TS AAC is ADTS and was given a second ADTS header; and a channel's timestamps were fed as the encoder wrote them, minutes or hours in when Tunarr already had the transcode running, to a pipeline that schedules from 0. Fixed here: `ProgressiveVideo` picks the framing from the extradata or the first packet, `progressive_aac_frame` passes ADTS through, and a channel's first Load rebases its first keyframe (`player::engine::first_open_rebases`). Regression tests: `ff::live_ts_tests`, `player::engine::first_open_rebase_tests`, `route::decision::live_route_tests`.
- **Mid-stream frame-rate change on a Tunarr channel.** Known from the design (`docs/live-tv-plan.md`, risk 2): the frame rate is probed once at tune. Not measured. Tunarr's frame-rate normalisation avoids it.
- **The clock's and the guide's time zone** on a television whose native process sees UTC. Not measured.

## Deliberately not done
